## 1. Company

Müller Maschinenbau GmbH, Wetzlar, builds special-purpose machines (assembly cells, testing rigs, machining lines) for the automotive supply industry. Founded 1979, 85 employees at one site. Owner-managed by Thomas Müller (68), sole shareholder and managing director. Customer of MainBank AG since 2015. Relationship manager (Markt): Martin Scholz. Credit analyst (Marktfolge): Dr. Petra Hoffmann.

## 2. Business model and special characteristics

Every machine is built to order and invoiced only after plant acceptance, mostly in Q4, when the automotive suppliers install new lines during the year-end shutdown. Material and wages are paid evenly over the year.

The result is seasonal liquidity: every year the liquidity in Q2 and Q3 falls 20-30% below the annual average. The dip is covered by the EUR 0.8 million working-capital line (Kontokorrent) and by the Q4 incoming payments, which bring the line back to zero every December. The pattern is structural, not a sign of distress.

Management style: hands-on and technical. Thomas Müller leads key negotiations and every final acceptance himself. No successor has been named.

## 5. Rating and watch points

Internal rating (RatingTool): 3, within the v2 range for new business (ratings 1-5).

- Equity ratio 28%: amber (20-30%), above the 20% minimum but with little buffer after the 2026 investment.
- Seasonal business: the Risk Assessment Checklist requires a 12-month liquidity plan and a comparison with the same quarters of the previous three years.
- Financial statements: the FY2025 statements were due within 9 months after year end, i.e. by 2026-09-30. From 2026-10-01 this is a warning sign, and the 9-month rule of Credit Policy v2 is no longer met with the FY2024 statements. The EUR 500,000 request needs the audited FY2025 statements or an approved EXC-REQ.
- Soft sign (applies): owner older than 60 without a documented succession plan. Thomas Müller is 68 and the succession is unresolved. This is the most important long-term risk of the relationship.

No hard warning signs (overdraft beyond the limit, auditor qualification, arrears, change of auditor) identified.
